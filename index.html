Before you give us your evenings

What Mend is not.

Read this part properly.

  • Not therapy, and not a therapist. Mend is a self-guided tool for reflection and repair. It does not diagnose, treat, or counsel, and it is not a substitute for care from a licensed professional. If you're working with one, bring your logs — that's the best use of this.
  • Not a crisis service. If anyone in your home is in danger right now, close this and call your local emergency number.
  • Not built to help you win. The planned app will not help anyone script, pressure, monitor, or manage another person's behaviour — including a partner's.
  • Not a way to sell your private words. The planned app's logs will need a clear, reviewed privacy policy before anyone is asked to use them. Today, the website collects no logs.
  • Not silent about harm. The planned safety response will direct people to real resources if a log suggests a child is being hurt. That response must be reviewed before the app ships.

Questions people actually ask

Fair enough.

Isn't this just telling me I'm a bad parent, with extra steps?

No. The planned design has no score, streak, or red days. The aim is to make room for a repair tonight and help you notice patterns later, without turning a difficult moment into a grade.

Why not just use Calm or Headspace?

Use what helps you. Mend is being designed for a narrower moment: after you've lost your temper and are deciding how to go back into the room. The app is still in development.

Will there be AI in this? What would it see?

The current plan includes software-assisted repair language and a weekly pattern view. The app does not exist yet, so there are no user logs to process. Before release, the privacy policy and safety behavior will need independent review and a clear explanation here.

Could my partner see my logs?

There are no logs today. The planned app will not have a shared family account. Its access controls still need to be built and tested before we can promise how they work.

What if my anger isn't about my kids?

Mend is being designed for one situation: a parent trying to repair after losing their temper with a child. It may not be the right tool for other situations.

Who's building this?

I'm Amir, one person in North Vancouver. I'm 22 and I don't have children. The about page explains why I'm building this. I plan to have a registered clinical counsellor review the app's language before release, but no reviewer has been signed yet.
